HERMETIC TEMPLE

A Practical Companion for Hermetic Initiation

The Companion

That which is above is like that which is below, to accomplish the miracles of the One Thing.

This is a practical companion to the classic work of Hermetic initiation. It gathers the elemental principles, the progressive steps of training, and daily practices for mind, soul, and body.

The work begins with equilibrium of the four elements. Mastery of thought, introspection, conscious breath, and conscious nourishment form the foundation. The body must be brought into healthy order before one proceeds to direct work with the electric and magnetic fluids. Higher steps build upon what has become habit.

Begin with the Elements. Then enter the Steps.

The Ten Steps

Each step contains mental, psychic (astral), and physical training. Progress only when the previous step is stable.

Step I

Mental: Thought control — observe, discipline, and subordinate thoughts.
Psychic: Introspection; create the black and white mirrors of the soul.
Physical: Conscious breathing, conscious reception of food, the magic of water.

Step II

Mental: Autosuggestion; concentration on the five senses.
Psychic: Elemental balance of the soul; transmutation of qualities.
Physical: Conscious pore breathing; body control in daily life.

Step III

Mental: Concentration with multiple senses; on objects, places, beings.
Psychic: Inhaling the elements into the whole body.
Physical: Accumulation of vital power through lungs and pores.

Step IV

Mental: Transplantation of consciousness into objects, animals, people.
Psychic: Accumulation of elements in body and parts; elemental harmony.
Physical: Ritual gesture, posture, and finger positions.

Step V

Mental: Space magic.
Psychic: Projection of elements outward.
Physical: Preparation for passive communication.

Steps VI–X

Higher work: mastery of Akasa, creation of beings, development of astral senses, mental wandering, fluid condensers, magic mirrors, separation of the astral body, and conscious communication with the personal God. These require firm foundation in the earlier steps.

Order of Work

The body must first be brought into elemental equilibrium and healthy order. Only then is it safe and effective to work directly with the electric and magnetic fluids (the active and passive polarities of fire and water). Premature manipulation of the fluids without a balanced body leads to disharmony. Complete the physical and psychic foundations of the early steps before advancing to fluid accumulation, projection, or higher applications.

The Elements

All that is created arises through the elements. Akasa is their source.

Fire

Heat and expansion. Electrical fluid. Active, constructive or destructive. Corresponds to will, vitality, the head.

Air

Mediator between fire and water. Warmth and humidity. Motion and balance. Corresponds to the chest and breath.

Water

Cold and contraction. Magnetic fluid. Nourishing or dissolving. Corresponds to emotion, the abdomen, fluids.

Earth

Solidification. Electromagnetic. Gives form, measure, and weight. Corresponds to the whole body, bones, and flesh.

Akasa

The ethereal principle. Spaceless, timeless, the causal source from which the four arise. Present in the blood and seed as vitality. The fifth power.

Daily Practices

Thought Observation (Step I)

Sit for 10–20 minutes. Watch thoughts arise and pass without following or suppressing them. Note the dominant quality (fiery, airy, watery, earthy). Return attention to the breath when carried away.

Soul Mirrors (Step I)

In the journal, list qualities. Black mirror: habits and traits that hinder equilibrium. White mirror: qualities already present that support it. Review and refine weekly. This is the foundation of self-knowledge.

Conscious Breathing

Breathe slowly and fully. On the inhalation, draw in the quality of one element (begin with the one you feel weakest). On the exhalation, release tension. 10–15 minutes. Later, extend to pore breathing: imagine the element entering through every pore.

Elemental Equilibrium

Visualize and feel each element in turn: Fire in the solar plexus or head, Air in the chest, Water in the abdomen, Earth in the legs and base. Spend equal time. Note imbalance and return to the weaker element.

This equilibrium of the body is the necessary foundation. The electric fluid (fire) and magnetic fluid (water) are worked with only after the body has been brought into order through the early steps.

Concentration (Step II onward)

Choose one sense. Hold a simple image, sound, sensation, scent, or taste steadily without distraction. Begin with short periods and lengthen. Combine senses only after single-sense mastery.

Nourishment

Food and breath supply the elements. A mixed diet preserves harmony. One-sided eating creates imbalance that later appears as disease or craving.

Conscious Reception of Food

Before eating, pause. Acknowledge the elements in the meal. Eat slowly, without distraction. Feel the food become part of the body. This is the magic of nourishment.

Elemental Correspondences in Food

Fire: warming spices, stimulating foods, foods that increase vitality and movement.
Air: light, fresh foods; moderation prevents excess.
Water: juicy fruits, liquids, cooling and moistening foods.
Earth: root vegetables, grains, denser sustaining foods.

Observe cravings: thirst may indicate excess fire; hunger, air; cold, water; tiredness, earth. Adjust gently toward balance rather than extreme restriction.

The Magic of Water

Water carries impression. Drink water with attention. A simple practice: hold a glass, breathe a desired quality into it (calm, clarity, strength), then drink consciously.

Meditations & Atmosphere

Longer elemental sittings and supporting music. Sit upright, breathe slowly, and remain with the quality.

Fire — 15–20 minutes

Inhale warmth and expansion into the solar plexus. Feel the electrical quality. On the exhale, release tension without losing the heat. When the mind wanders, return to the sensation of fire.

Air — 15–20 minutes

Inhale lightness and clarity into the chest. Feel the mediating quality between expansion and contraction. Let thoughts arise and pass like wind. Return to the breath as the still center.

Water — 15–20 minutes

Inhale coolness and receptivity into the abdomen. Feel the magnetic quality. Allow emotion to be present without being carried away. On the exhale, release what no longer serves.

Earth — 15–20 minutes

Inhale solidity and weight into the legs and base. Feel the body as the vessel that holds the other three. Rest in stability. When restlessness appears, return to the sensation of earth.

Full Equilibrium — 20–30 minutes

Cycle through all four elements in sequence, spending equal time with each. End by resting in the center where they meet. This is the foundation before any work with the electric and magnetic fluids.

The Seven Hermetic Principles

From The Kybalion. These are the laws that govern the universe and the work of development.

1. The Principle of Mentalism

“The All is Mind; the Universe is Mental.”

All that exists is a mental creation of the All. The universe is held in the mind of the One. To understand this is to recognize that thought is the primary substance and that mastery of mind is mastery of conditions.

2. The Principle of Correspondence

“As above, so below; as below, so above.”

There is harmony and correspondence between the planes of existence — mental, astral, and physical. Patterns repeat across scales. What is true of the greater is true of the lesser. This law allows knowledge of the unseen by study of the seen.

3. The Principle of Vibration

“Nothing rests; everything moves; everything vibrates.”

From the densest matter to the highest spirit, all is in motion. Differences between manifestations are differences in rate of vibration. To change one’s rate is to change one’s condition and influence.

4. The Principle of Polarity

“Everything is dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites.”

Opposites are identical in nature, differing only in degree. Heat and cold, love and hate, light and dark are the same continuum. Mastery lies in the ability to transmute along the scale rather than being fixed at one pole.

5. The Principle of Rhythm

“Everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; all things rise and fall.”

The pendulum swing is constant. Action and reaction, advance and retreat, are law. The trained mind learns to rise above the swing or to use its rhythm consciously instead of being carried by it.

6. The Principle of Cause and Effect

“Every cause has its effect; every effect has its cause.”

Nothing escapes the law. What is called chance is unrecognized cause. The magician becomes a conscious cause rather than a passive effect, initiating chains of causation instead of merely receiving them.

7. The Principle of Gender

“Gender is in everything; everything has its masculine and feminine principles.”

The masculine is projective, the feminine receptive. Both are present on every plane and required for generation. Creation occurs through their interaction. Neither is complete alone.

These seven principles are the foundation upon which the elemental work and the steps of initiation rest.
